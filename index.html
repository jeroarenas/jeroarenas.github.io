Software & data

Open tools and resources from our work on topic modeling, language models and public-sector text. Most were built with domain experts and released with the paper that describes them.

LLMsInformation extractionPublic procurement

PONTE / pAtChWoRK

A hybrid NLP and LLM pipeline that turns public procurement PDFs into a navigable knowledge base: it corrects classification errors, extracts unstructured fields and computes procurement indicators. pAtChWoRK is the first, openly available version, evaluated in a user study with procurement practitioners; PONTE extends it and is being integrated into the procurement platform of the ALIA initiative.

CASE

Large-scale exploitation of topic models: a Solr engine with a custom indexing strategy for Bayesian and neural topic models, topic inference on new texts and topic-enriched semantic search, packaged with Docker. Used for STI indicators and for a service that supports the evaluation of research projects.

ITMT · Interactive Topic Model Trainer

A graphical tool for domain experts to train, evaluate, annotate and curate topic models, covering Bayesian and neural methods, including hierarchical models. Developed in the IntelComp project and released under the MIT license.

Topic models for decision-support systems

A two-part study of how to train, represent, exploit and refine topic models so that they are useful in decision-support systems, including expert-in-the-loop hierarchical topic models that are model-agnostic and easy to tune.

Statistical learningSemi-supervisedReproducible

SS-unbiased

When does knowing the input covariance help least squares? Finite-sample expressions for the excess mean-square error of least squares and of unbiased semi-supervised estimators that use the known covariance, and a realizable leave-one-out combination that attains the optimum. Work started with the late Jan Larsen and Lars Kai Hansen at DTU. The repository reproduces all results of the paper.

Models and data on Zenodo

About

I am Full Professor at the Department of Signal Theory and Communications of Universidad Carlos III de Madrid and a member of the Machine Learning for Data Science group. My background is in statistical signal processing (adaptive filtering, multivariate analysis, kernel methods); for the last decade I have worked on the semantic analysis of large text collections, from topic models to language models, always with an eye on the people who use the results.

Much of this work is done with Lorena Calvo-Bartolomé, Saúl Blanco-Fortes and colleagues at UC3M, and with collaborators at the University of Maryland, the Technical University of Denmark and the Spanish public administration.
